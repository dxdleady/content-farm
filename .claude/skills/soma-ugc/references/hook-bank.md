# The hook bank

Sixty hooks for the next fifty posts, plus the rules that decide which ones are safe to write.
Researched 2026-08-27 against TikTok's published policy, FTC rule text, and real customer
language. Everything quoted below is verbatim from a source; nothing here is invented tone.

---

## Read this first: the persona is a legal problem, not a style choice

All 36 shipped posts are **first-person testimony from a character who does not exist**:
*"I slept five hours"*, *"Six weeks in, the scale barely moved"*, *"I don't choose my
workouts anymore"*, *"I haven't planned a dinner in weeks"*.

**16 CFR Part 465** (the FTC's Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, in force since
21 October 2024) makes it a violation to disseminate a testimonial that misrepresents

> "(1) That the reviewer or testimonialist exists; (2) That the reviewer or testimonialist
> used or otherwise had experience with the product…; or (3) The reviewer's or
> testimonialist's experience with the product"

The FTC's own announcement names the case directly: *"reviews and testimonials that
misrepresent that they are by someone who does not exist, **such as AI-generated fake
reviews**, or who did not have actual experience with the business."* Civil penalties run to
**$53,088 per violation** (16 CFR § 1.98). The Endorsement Guides add § 255.1(e): an
endorser is liable *"when an endorser falsely represents that they personally used a
product."*

**The fix is a register change, not a smaller font disclaimer.** Three lanes stay open:

| lane | what the voice is doing | example |
|---|---|---|
| **Demonstration** | showing the product working; the screen is the evidence, not a person's word | "It didn't give me a score. It gave me a sentence." → the why-card |
| **Education** | teaching something true about training, sleep or wearables, product as the tool at the end | "HRV in plain English" |
| **Observation / second person** | describing the viewer's situation, never claiming a personal result | "Your watch has been recording this for two years" |

**Closed:** first-person outcome claims ("six weeks in and X changed for me"), before/after
framing, and anything where the persona vouches for a result. If a founder or a real
customer says it on the record, it is a testimonial and it is fine — from the avatar it is
not.

This also cleans up a second exposure: FTC's DoNotPay order bars *"making claims about its
ability to substitute for any professional service without evidence"* — which is exactly
what `deck-12seconds` ("my last trainer took three sessions") flirts with.

## What the platform actually punishes

The dominant outcome is **not** deletion. TikTok's own EU transparency report, H1 2025:
**24,534,707 removals against 169,527,678 restrictions** — silent de-recommendation is ~7×
more likely than a takedown, it arrives with no notice, and the analytics tab is the only
place it shows.

**Named in TikTok's For You feed standards as deliberately de-repeated:** *"extreme fitness
or dieting, sexual suggestiveness, sadness, or overgeneralized mental health advice."* An
account that posts one shape daily is throttled **by design, not by a strike** — which is
why the next fifty must rotate format, not just wording.

**The sentence to keep taped to the wall**, verbatim from TikTok's Community Guidelines,
listing what IS allowed:

> "Fitness routines and nutrition focused on sports or general athleticism, **not extreme
> weight loss**."

### Do not write

| never | why |
|---|---|
| kg/lb lost, or any timeframe on a body change | "unrealistic weight loss results" = removal; "fast or dramatic results" = FYF-ineligible |
| "calorie deficit", a calorie number, fasting windows | "Restrictive low-calorie diets, including extended intermittent fasting" = FYF-ineligible |
| before/after body photos | listed as misleading visuals; removal risk |
| "snatched", "flat stomach", "get your body back" | body-type-as-ideal |
| "if you're carrying extra weight…", anything scolding | "Insulting someone's physical attributes" = harassment |
| body-part-to-object comparisons | named explicitly as not allowed |
| "fixes your hormones", "heals your gut", any cure | health claim; needs RCT-grade substantiation under FTC's Health Products guidance |
| GLP-1 / Ozempic / "nature's Ozempic" | blanket ban on the Shop side, high risk elsewhere |

Safe axis: **consistency, routine, sleep, energy, stress, showing up, tracking, general
athleticism** — never size, weight, calories, or speed of change.

## Two mechanics worth more than any single hook

**1. The app name goes in the pinned comment, never on slide 1.** Cal AI (8.3M downloads),
Musa (10M views / 30K downloads in a month) and Vent Now all withhold the name from the
post and deliver it in comment replies. The failure mode is documented too — a competitor
account did 15M views with no name and no pinned comment: *"the viral part is already done,
so these two small fixes would turn it into a ton of downloads."*

**2. Slideshows are not a reach cheat code.** The "5× reach" claim circulating on vendor
blogs is backwards: Metricool's 2.3M-post study found **video gets 5× the reach and 6× the
interactions** of images, and image publishing volume grew ~140% year on year, so the format
got *more* crowded. Slideshows earn their place on **cost per attempt, saves, and comments**
— and comments are where the app name gets delivered. Questions get **+26.19% comments**;
statements get more reach. Pick per post which one the job needs.

Other measured details: hooks land at **5–8 words**, twelve at the outside. Unround numbers
("47", "1,247") beat round ones. Shock/surprise is the **most used and worst performing**
hook type. "POV:", "watch until the end" and "no one is talking about" now read as
saturation tells.

## The eight formulas, with what they actually did

| # | formula | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Recurring shared-symptom question** — "anyone else…?" about a real, cyclical experience | Flo ran four near-identical hooks across four months: 1.8M / 3.6M / 3.2M / 1.4M views, 1.6K–13K comments each. Re-runnable forever |
| 2 | **Two-slide tension → release** — one line the viewer has said out loud, then the screen that answers it | Stronger: "I don't feel like going to the gym today" → **48M views**; "you've changed" → 11.1M. App Store #432 → #14 in a month |
| 3 | **Weird / credentialed hack list** — counterintuitive, sensory, attributed to a professional | 6.4M views across 22 posts on one template; "weird" itself is now a tell, rotate the modifier |
| 4 | **Numbered mistake list** — "N mistakes killing your X" | 37× the creator's own baseline, ~5% save rate. Negative framing beats the positive version of the same idea |
| 5 | **Ranking / countdown** — roast honestly, crown yourself last | Symmetry: **83M views**, #1 in Spain's App Store, 112K saves on a single post. Ranked #2 of 10 hook types by average views *and* uncrowded |
| 6 | **Identity / grievance callout** — name an enemy that is not a body | Musa: "10 years of using a male designed period app and now I find this…" — 1.2M views, 4.8K bookmarks |
| 7 | **Validation before data** — three slides in the viewer's own messy words, then what the app knows | "The payoff felt like relief rather than information" — structurally the safest formula here: it makes no claim until the data slide |
| 8 | **Confession / imperfection** | Worth ~1 in 8 posts. AI-labelled *emotional* content takes the biggest measured engagement hit, so a genuinely human note is the most defensible asset the account has |

## Retire these

Counted across the 36 shipped decks. Anything in the next fifty that repeats them buys
nothing:

**protein-first** (11 decks, verbatim-identical in four) · **same bedtime / "sleep is
training"** (21) · **walk after meals** (14) · **water before coffee** (11) · **"stop
guessing" / "built for someone else's body"** (~12 CTAs and the whole thesis of 4 decks) ·
the **`N — Claim` listicle as the default shape** (16 of 36).

Also retire the closing frame: **all 36 CTAs use the same screenshot and one of three
headline shapes.** The last slide of the account has never changed. Vary it — a question, a
single line of copy, a different screen, a pinned-comment prompt.
