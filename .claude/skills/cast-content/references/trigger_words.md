# Trigger Words

Trigger words amplify emotional impact and can increase engagement by ~10x. Use 1-2 per hook for maximum viral potential.

> Adapted from a general viral-hook reference written for personal brands. The four
> categories, the word banks and the rules are the source's. Every example has been
> rewritten for a **product account** — (cast) is a tool, not a person, and a product
> speaking as "I" is a fiction the reader can feel. Pattern names map to the moves in
> `hooks.md`.

---

## Category 1: Insider Words

**Purpose:** Create exclusivity, scarcity, and "insider knowledge" feeling. Makes readers feel they're getting privileged information.

**Best paired with:** the unexpected, myth-busting, admission

**Word bank:**
secretly, quietly, discovered, revealed, uncovered, hidden, actually, underground, behind-the-scenes, confession, leaked, exposed, unspoken, overlooked, buried, invisible, ignored, unpublished, undisclosed, internal, private

**Use with care.** This is the class that curdles into hype fastest, because it promises a
secret. It only works when there is a real specific behind it — "hidden truth" is nothing,
"the pass nobody mentions" is something.

### Examples in Action

**Move: The Unexpected**
- ❌ Weak: "There are more steps than people think"
- ✅ Strong: "The four passes **nobody mentions** when they say 'just edit it'"

**Move: Myth-busting**
- ❌ Weak: "Room tone is a problem people miss"
- ✅ Strong: "The noise you stopped hearing an hour ago is **still buried** in the file"

**Move: Admission**
- ❌ Weak: "We used to get this wrong"
- ✅ Strong: "The episode we **quietly** shelved, and the four hours that killed it"

## Category 2: Helper Words

**Purpose:** Highlight pain points, losses, and urgency. Triggers loss aversion (fear of missing out or continuing to lose).

**Best paired with:** loss, admission, problem→fix decks, tiny change big impact

**Word bank:**
losing, wasting, costing, stealing, killing, sabotaging, destroying, bleeding, leaking, draining, missing, sacrificing, throwing away, burning, hemorrhaging, squandering, forfeiting

**The strongest class for this product.** The audience is losing hours right now and has
stopped noticing — that is the entire pitch, and this is the vocabulary for it.

### Examples in Action

**Move: Loss**
- ❌ Weak: "Editing takes a long time"
- ✅ Strong: "Your edit is **stealing** an evening a week"

**Move: Admission**
- ❌ Weak: "We shipped an episode late once"
- ✅ Strong: "One tangent **burned** four hours and the episode never went out"

**Move: Tiny Change, Big Impact**
- ❌ Weak: "One transcript pass saves time"
- ✅ Strong: "One transcript pass stops you **wasting** four of the nine"

**Key difference:**
- "Losing time" = generic, forgettable
- "Bleeding 12 hours" = visceral, shareable

## Category 3: Thinker Words

**Purpose:** Challenge assumptions, create intrigue, and position content as contrarian or counterintuitive. Makes readers question what they know.

**Best paired with:** myth-busting, everything wrong, reset, less beats more

**Word bank:**
backwards, opposite, counterintuitive, paradox, irony, myth, misconception, illusion, fallacy, contradiction, reversal, upside-down, inverse, contrarian, unconventional, paradoxical, misunderstood

### Examples in Action

**Move: Myth-busting**
- ❌ Weak: "A better microphone will not fix bad audio"
- ✅ Strong: "The **biggest myth** about podcast audio is that it starts at the mic"

**Move: Less Beats More**
- ❌ Weak: "Fewer edits can sound better than more gear"
- ✅ Strong: "The **paradox**: fewer edits beat a better microphone, every time"

**Move: Reset**
- ❌ Weak: "Forget what you know about audio editing"
- ✅ Strong: "Everything you learned in a video timeline is a **misconception** here"

**Move: Everything Wrong**
- ❌ Weak: "People edit in an inefficient order"
- ✅ Strong: "Your whole edit is **inverted** — you are searching by ear"

## Category 4: Amplifiers

**Purpose:** Intensify any message or pattern. Use with other trigger words or standalone to boost emotional impact.

**Mix with any category or move for intensity boost**

**Word bank:**
every, all, none, always, never, only, biggest, worst, zero, entirely, completely, totally, absolutely, literally, exactly, precisely

**A real number is the better amplifier where one exists.** "Nine passes" beats "so many
passes" and costs fewer characters.

### Examples in Action

**Amplifier + Insider Word**
- ❌ Weak: "There is a step most shows skip"
- ✅ Strong: "The **only** pass **every** weekly show **quietly** dropped"

**Amplifier + Helper Word**
- ❌ Weak: "You waste time on filler words"
- ✅ Strong: "You're **burning** two hours a month on the word 'um' **alone**"

**Amplifier + Thinker Word**
- ❌ Weak: "The usual advice is wrong"
- ✅ Strong: "The mic advice is **completely backwards**. The fix is in the transcript"

**Standalone Amplifier Usage**
- ❌ Weak: "Not much of the work is creative"
- ✅ Strong: "**None** of it was the creative part"

## Integration Guidelines

### DO:
- ✅ Integrate naturally during hook creation (not as an afterthought)
- ✅ Match trigger word category to the move
- ✅ Use 1-2 trigger words per hook (more = diluted impact)
- ✅ Combine amplifiers with other categories for maximum effect
- ✅ Use specific numbers with trigger words ("bleeding 12 hours" not "bleeding time")
- ✅ Test: Does the trigger word make the hook feel more compelling?
- ✅ Count the characters afterwards — see the budget table in `hooks.md`

### DON'T:
- ❌ Force trigger words awkwardly into hooks
- ❌ Use multiple trigger words from same category in one hook
- ❌ Use trigger words without specific details (e.g., "secretly revealed" needs context)
- ❌ Overuse amplifiers (one per hook maximum)
- ❌ Mix incompatible word types (e.g., "secretly bleeding" rarely works)
- ❌ Let a long verb cost a line — "hemorrhaging" spends 12 of about 45 characters
- ❌ Reach for a trigger word to rescue a hook with no fact underneath. That is what the
      word cannot fix; go back to the topic

### On intensity and the brand

`voice.avoid` bans **claims**, not volume: "ad-safe", "brand-safe", "copyright-proof",
"guaranteed zero copyright claims". Every entry is a factual assertion with legal exposure.
None of them is about being loud. Write with as much force as the words allow — just never
make those four claims while doing it.

## Quick Reference: Move-to-Trigger-Word Matching

| Move | Best Trigger Words | Example |
|--------------|-------------------|---------|
| Loss | Helper + Amplifier | "You're **bleeding** an **entire** evening to nine passes" |
| Tiny Change | Helper | "One transcript pass stops you **wasting** four of the nine" |
| Myth-Busting | Thinker | "The **biggest myth** about podcast audio is **completely backwards**" |
| Everything Wrong | Thinker + Amplifier | "Your **whole** edit is **inverted** — you search by ear" |
| Less Beats More | Thinker | "The **paradox**: fewer edits beat a better microphone" |
| Reset | Thinker | "Everything you learned in a video timeline is a **misconception**" |
| The Unexpected | Insider + Amplifier | "The **only** pass **every** weekly show dropped" |
| Admission | Helper | "One tangent **burned** four hours and the episode never shipped" |
| Number-led | the number itself | "**Nine** passes over the same hour" |

The source's table also matched Authority Credibility, Steal My Process, Proxy Learning,
Cautionary Tale and Data-Driven. Those all require a narrator with a biography or a dataset
we do not have — see the rejected moves in `hooks.md`. **Admission** is the brand-safe
replacement for Cautionary Tale, written in the first person *plural*.

## Impact Comparison

Understanding the measurable difference trigger words make:

| Without Trigger Words | With Trigger Words | Engagement Multiplier |
|-----------------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| "You're losing time on meetings" | "You're bleeding 12 hours every week on meetings" | ~10x shares |
| "I found patterns in viral posts" | "I uncovered the hidden patterns nobody talks about" | ~8x saves |
| "Common advice is wrong" | "The biggest myth is completely backwards" | ~12x engagement |
| "Here's my process" | "Steal my secretly tested process" | ~7x clicks |

**The difference is measurable.** Trigger words transform good hooks into viral hooks.

> These rows are the source's, left as they were rather than re-fitted to our examples —
> attaching someone else's multipliers to sentences nobody measured would be worse than
> quoting theirs. Take the direction, not the decimals, and never let a multiplier reach a
> slide: `scoring.md` flags every figure in copy for sourcing.
